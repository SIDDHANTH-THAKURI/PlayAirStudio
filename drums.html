How to play

The sticks

  • Point your index finger and you're holding a stick. Relax your hand and you've put it down — that is the whole control. Resting, gesturing, scratching your nose: all silent, with no special case for any of them.
  • The stick is drawn along the finger you're pointing with and carries on past it, and the tip is what hits. That makes it a lever: a small flick of the wrist swings the tip a long way, so a comfortable movement is plenty. You should not need to swing hard — swinging hard mostly just makes it loud.
  • The drum you're currently aimed at is ringed in your hand's colour, so you can see what you're about to hit before you commit to the stroke.
  • If the sticks feel too long or too short for the space you have, change Stick length. The tip is both what you aim with and what the detection watches, so the two never disagree.
  • Sit back far enough that the camera can see both hands with room to move. There is nothing to calibrate — the kit is already where it needs to be.

Playing

  • Swing at a drum and stop. The hit lands on the stop, which is where a real stick would make its sound, and how fast you were moving when you got there is how hard it lands.
  • Harder is brighter, not just louder. A ghost note on the snare and a full backbeat are two different sounds, the way they are on a real kit.
  • Both hands are independent, and each is drawn in its own colour so you can see which stick did what.
  • Hi-hat: hit the top disc for an open hat, the bottom one for a closed one. Hitting closed slams the open one shut, exactly as the pedal does.
  • Ride: the middle is the bell, the outside is the bow — the same as on the real thing.
  • Lift the stick between hits. A stroke needs somewhere to come from, so the stick has to come back up before it can land again, which is how drumming works anyway.

If hits aren't registering

  • Check the track figure in the header. Below about 20 detections a second a stroke can begin and finish between two looks at your hands, and no amount of tuning recovers it. Closing other GPU-heavy tabs and enabling hardware acceleration is the fix.
  • Make sure you're actually pointing. If no stick appears, the tracker isn't seeing a point — extend the index finger clearly and let the other three fall in. They don't have to make a fist, but they do have to be out of the way.
  • Stop, don't follow through. The detector is looking for a stick being brought up short, not for one travelling fast. A long lazy sweep across the kit is deliberately silent, which is what lets you move between drums without playing every one on the way.
  • If you're reaching uncomfortably far for anything, try a smaller Kit size. The stick tip hangs below your hand, so the kit sits lower in the frame than a photo of a kit would — that is deliberate, and it is what makes the top row reachable at all.
